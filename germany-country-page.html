01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "Germany is efficient and organized" — the specific, slightly awkward details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"Can I just tap my card everywhere?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No — Germany is still genuinely cash-reliant. Plenty of smaller cafés, markets, and even some doctors' offices are cash-only. Carry cash by default, don't assume a card will work.
"Is the recycling sorting really that strict?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Genuinely, yes — paper, plastic/packaging, organic waste, and general waste go in separate bins, and neighbours do notice if you get it consistently wrong. Learn your building's system in week one.
"Do shops really close on Sundays?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Almost entirely, yes — by law, most shops (including supermarkets) stay shut on Sundays. Only train-station shops, some bakeries, and petrol stations stay open. Do your grocery run by Saturday evening.
"Is the paperwork as intense as people say?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes, genuinely — registering your address (Anmeldung) within two weeks of arrival is legally required and unlocks almost everything else, from opening a bank account to getting a phone contract. Do it first, before anything else.
"Do I actually need German to get by?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
On campus, often not — many Master's programs teach entirely in English. Off campus, at the Bürgeramt or the pharmacy, basic German goes a long way and sometimes is the only option. Even A2-level makes daily life meaningfully easier.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The national visa, in plain language.
Not the full legal text — the numbers that actually decide whether you qualify, applied for through the German Embassy in Dhaka, not a centralized online portal.
BLOCKED ACCOUNT (SPERRKONTO) YOU'LL NEED
€11,904
≈ ৳16.8L · €992/month × 12 months, released monthly, not withdrawable in full at once
HOW THE BLOCKED ACCOUNT ACTUALLY WORKS
You deposit the full year's amount upfront into a Sperrkonto with a provider like Fintiba or Expatrio. Once you arrive in Germany and activate it, the bank releases exactly 1/12th per month — you cannot withdraw it all at once, by design.
National visa application fee: €75 for adults, paid at the embassy — separate from the blocked account and health insurance.
Most public universities charge €0–350/year in tuition (a semester administrative fee) — except Baden-Württemberg, which charges non-EU students roughly €1,500/semester. See the policy note.
Post-study work: graduates of a German university can get an 18-month job-seeker visa to find qualified employment, with unrestricted work rights during that search.
Checked against German Federal Foreign Office and Embassy Dhaka guidance, confirmed 2 Sep 2026
Genuinely unclear — verify directly before assuming
Does Bangladesh need an APS certificate? Sources disagree.
The APS (Akademische Prüfstelle) certificate verifies academic documents before some students can apply for a German student visa — it's a real, sometimes multi-week, sometimes non-trivial-fee process. It has always applied to India, China, and Vietnam. Whether it currently applies to Bangladesh is where guidance actually conflicts: some sources say Bangladesh is exempt entirely, using standard document legalisation instead; others list Bangladesh among the countries where it's now mandatory, citing an expansion since late 2022. We could not resolve this conflict from public sources with confidence, and we'd rather tell you that than guess. Before assuming either way, confirm directly with the German Embassy in Dhaka or check uni-assist and your specific university's admissions page — this is exactly the kind of requirement that's expensive to get wrong in either direction.
