01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
The practical details that tend to catch first-year students off guard, not a general portrait of the country.
"Is "Cyprus" the same as Northern Cyprus?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No. This guide is about the Republic of Cyprus, the EU member in the south. Agents also advertise universities in the north, which is administered separately. UCY's own page warns that the Republic's Migration Department will reject applications linked to money transactions in the north and calls the northern airport an illegal point of entry. Check which side a university is on before you pay a deposit.
"Will English be enough?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Mostly at the private universities and in some master's programmes, which teach in English. The University of Cyprus is different: its international-exams admission route asks for a Greek qualification (GCE Modern Greek or equivalent). Check each programme's teaching language, and expect shops, offices and landlords to switch between Greek and English.
"How hot does it really get?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Inland Nicosia averages a daily high of about 34°C in July and gets about 12 hours of sunshine a day. The coast is a little milder. Check that a flat has air-conditioning before you rent it, and ask what the electricity bill looks like in summer.
"Can I work from day one?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No. A revised decree published on 6 March 2026 says a student may work only after completing the first semester and enrolling in the second. The Department of Labour lists up to 20 hours a week during classes and 38 during breaks, in named sectors, on a contract it has certified. Breaking the rules can mean a fine or imprisonment.
"Is travel insurance enough?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No. The residence permit needs a certificate of health insurance for inpatient and outpatient care and the transport of remains (called "Plan A"), valid for a year or your stay if shorter. UNIC charges about €175 a year for its health and accident cover; other sources quote €120–350.
"Do I need a flat before I land?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
You need one soon after. For the residence permit, UCY asks for a rental agreement certified by the president of a community council and signed by two Cypriot witnesses. Banks want the same document to open an account, and the permit asks for a Cypriot bank confirmation. Universities have only a limited number of rooms.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The Cypriot student permit, in plain language.
Non-EU students need an entry permit before travelling and a residence permit after arriving. These are the numbers and deadlines that decide whether you qualify, plus one set of work rules to read first.
FUNDS YOU HAVE TO SHOW
€7,000 minimum balance
AFTER A MASTER'S OR HIGHER: UP TO 12 MONTHS TO LOOK FOR WORK OR START A BUSINESS
Each square is one month of the post-study permit the Migration Department describes. It needs a completed master's degree or higher (EQF level 7), so a bachelor's alone does not qualify, and the job or business must match the level of your studies. The official page is undated, so confirm the current terms before you plan around it.
How it works: your university submits the entry-permit application to the Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD). You send certified documents to the university first, and you stay in Bangladesh until the entry permit (the "blue slip") is issued. We could not confirm which Cypriot mission, if any, handles Bangladeshi applicants in person, so ask your university whether an interview or embassy visit is needed.
Fees: CRMD lists €70 for the entry-permit and residence-permit application, €55 to issue or renew a permit and €70 for first registration in the Aliens' Register. Universities add their own visa-processing fee, for example €150 at UNIC and €350 at Neapolis.
Funds: set by each university, not nationally. One university's procedure asks for a €7,000 minimum balance on a statement from the month before submission, and other sources quote €7,000–8,000. After arrival, UCY asks for a Cypriot bank confirmation of at least €800 for the permit period (about ৳110,600).
Documents: a police certificate, a medical certificate (tests for tuberculosis, hepatitis B and C, HIV and syphilis), attested academic records and a bank statement, with translations where needed. Some universities repeat the medical tests after you arrive.
After you land: within 7 days you register and apply for the residence permit. You also need Plan A health insurance and a certified rental agreement. The CRMD says complete entry-permit applications are examined within 1 month and residence-permit applications within 3 months, though UCY says the card takes about 4 months. The permit lasts up to a year and is renewed annually, with a check on academic progress.
Staying abroad: UCY says your permit is cancelled automatically if you stay outside Cyprus for more than 3 months, so ask your university about a re-entry permit before you travel for long breaks.
Checked against the Migration Department (CRMD), the Department of Labour, UCY's International Office and Cypriot press, 18 Oct 2026
Work and stay rules · 2026
You can't work in your first semester, and the post-study permit needs a master's.
A revised decree published in the Official Gazette on 6 March 2026 says a student may work only after completing the first academic semester and enrolling in the second. The Department of Labour's page lists up to 20 hours a week during classes and 38 during breaks, in named sectors such as hotels, restaurants, retail, delivery and cleaning, on a contract the Department has certified. Breaking the rules can bring a fine or imprisonment. After you graduate, the Migration Department describes a permit of up to 12 months to look for work or start a business, but only for students who completed a master's or higher. A bachelor's graduate would need another route. We have not seen the full text of the revised decree, so confirm the hours and sectors with your university and the Department of Labour.

07 / FUNDING THAT FITS
Scholarships worth actually applying for.
Funding for Bangladeshi students in Cyprus mostly comes from the universities themselves, as tuition reductions rather than full scholarships. Plan on paying most of your costs.

UNIC academic merit scholarships
University of Nicosia · First-year reduction by high-school grades, then 50%, 20% or 10% by GPA
Full-time study only · not available for most postgraduate or medical programmes · confirm the current terms with UNIC admissions
UNIC financial aid for international students
University of Nicosia · Tuition reduction for students from underprivileged backgrounds, scored on socioeconomic criteria
Apply through UNIC admissions on its international-students aid form · amounts vary
UCLan Cyprus bursary
UCLan Cyprus · Reported €950 a year off undergraduate fees and €1,000 off a 90-ECTS postgraduate programme
From a secondary source (October 2026) · confirm with UCLan Cyprus
Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's
European Union · Joint master's run by university consortia; some include Cypriot partners
Open to students worldwide · apply to each programme and check its own scholarship terms and deadline (not checked for the 2027 intake)
Aga Khan Foundation International Scholarship Programme
Aga Khan Foundation · 50% grant and 50% loan for postgraduate study
Not Cyprus-specific · check the foundation's eligible-country list and requirements
We found no Cypriot government scholarship open to Bangladeshi nationals. UNIC's CyprusAid scheme is limited to applicants from ten African countries, the University of Cyprus's 2024 postgraduate scholarship round was aimed at applicants from Arab countries and has closed, and UNIC's government grants are for Cypriot and EU citizens. Do not count on funding when you show your funds for the permit.
