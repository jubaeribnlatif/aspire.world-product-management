01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "Canada is friendly and welcoming" — the specific, slightly awkward details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"Is 'sorry' actually an apology here?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Rarely. "Sorry" is closer to "excuse me" here — said when someone bumps into you, when you bump into them, even when neither of you did anything wrong. It's filler, not guilt.
"How much winter gear do I actually need?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
More than you think, and it's not optional. A coat rated for -20°C or colder, real boots, and layers underneath — this isn't Dhaka winter with more wind, it's a genuinely different climate.
"Does Canada work the same everywhere?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No — Quebec runs its own immigration process alongside the federal one, with French as the primary language in daily life. If Montreal's on your list, budget real time to understand the CAQ process separately.
"Do I need to tip like in the US?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Pretty much, yes — 15–18% at sit-down restaurants is standard. The one genuine difference: sales tax isn't included in the sticker price anywhere, so the bill at checkout is always higher than the number on the shelf.
"Is small talk with strangers normal here?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Genuinely, yes — chatting with a stranger in a checkout line or on a bus isn't weird here, it's the norm. Politeness runs both ways: it's real, and it's also often how casual local networking actually happens.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The study permit, in plain language.
Not the full legal text — the numbers that actually decide whether you qualify, and one system change worth understanding before you apply.
PROOF OF FUNDS YOU'LL NEED TO SHOW
$23,448
≈ ৳20.8L · living expenses only, for one year — separate from tuition and travel costs
HOW FUNDING PROOF ACTUALLY WORKS
No fixed holding period like some other countries. Show it via a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC), a student loan letter, or 6 months of bank statements (recently increased from 4) with a clear balance history. Bank drafts are no longer accepted.
Study permit fee: $150, plus $85 biometrics — $235 total in core government fees, separate from tuition and proof of funds.
Most applicants now need a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) confirming a space within your province's capped allocation — see the policy note for who's exempt.
Post-study work: the PGWP matches your program length, up to 3 years for Master's graduates — degree holders are exempt from the field-of-study restrictions affecting diploma programs.
Checked against IRCC guidance, confirmed 2 Sep 2026
Confirmed policy change — already in effect
The study permit cap changed how you apply. Master's students get a real exemption.
Since 2024, Canada caps how many study permit applications it accepts each year — 309,670 spaces for 2026 requiring a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL), out of roughly 408,000 total permits issued across all categories. Most applicants now need this document from their province before applying at all. The genuinely good news for this platform's audience: Master's and doctoral students at a public institution no longer need a PAL, as of 1 January 2026 — the exemption that matters most if you're applying at that level. Separately, the Student Direct Stream — the old expedited pathway — has been abolished entirely; everyone now goes through the standard process. Proof-of-funds requirements also rose again on 1 September 2026, to $23,448. This is a genuinely different system than it was two years ago, not a minor update.
