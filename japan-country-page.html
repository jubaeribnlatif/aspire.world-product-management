01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "Japan is a beautiful blend of tradition and technology" — the specific, practical details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"Do I really need to take my shoes off?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes — homes, most dorms, many restaurants, and even some school buildings have a genkan entryway where street shoes come off. Slip-on shoes save you a lot of morning fumbling.
"Is sorting trash actually a big deal?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Genuinely, yes. Burnable, non-burnable, recyclables and PET bottles each have fixed collection days set by your ward — getting it wrong is one of the fastest ways to hear from a neighbor or landlord.
"Should I tip for great service?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No — tipping isn't part of the culture at all. Excellent service is already the default, and leaving money behind can confuse or even embarrass staff rather than please them.
"Are convenience stores really that useful?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Extremely. 7-Eleven, Lawson and FamilyMart handle bill payments, ATM cash, parcel pickup, printing and genuinely good food — open 24/7 and often the fastest way to solve a random errand.
"Do I need to register somewhere after I land?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes — you're issued a Residence Card at the airport if your stay is over 3 months, then must register your address at your local ward office within 14 days. Your university's international office usually walks you through it.
"Is it true no one talks on the train?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Not a law, but a strong norm — phone calls on trains are considered rude, and most carriages ride in near silence. Texting and reading are fine; talking on the phone draws stares.
02 / A YEAR IN THE LIFE
Japan's year doesn't start where you'd expect.
Tap a season — the academic year begins in April, not September, and that changes how you should plan your first few months.
Spring
MARCH — MAY
The academic year — and most job and lease cycles — start on 1 April, not in autumn. Cherry blossoms (sakura) peak for about two weeks in late March/early April, right as orientation and hanami parties collide.
Pack for it: Light layers, and don't be surprised by cedar pollen allergy season (kafun-shō) — pharmacies sell masks and eye drops for it everywhere.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The student visa, in plain language.
Not the full legal text — the numbers and deadlines that actually decide whether you qualify.
YOUR PART-TIME WORK LIMIT
28 hours / week
Across any 7 consecutive days, combining all part-time jobs — and only after you've applied for work permission. Only available after your first year enrolled.
REGISTER YOUR ADDRESS WITHIN 14 DAYS OF ARRIVAL
Your Residence Card is issued at the airport on arrival — the 14-day clock starts the moment you land, and your local ward office handles registration, not the university alone.
Visa fee: free for Bangladeshi nationals — confirmed directly by the Embassy of Japan in Dhaka. The main cost is the Certificate of Eligibility process itself, which is free to file but takes time.
Certificate of Eligibility (COE): your university applies on your behalf; approval typically takes 1–3 months, and is valid for only 3 months once issued — your visa and travel need to land inside that window.
Visa issuance: once you submit the COE at the embassy, the standard turnaround is about 5 business days — assuming no extra document requests.
Checked against the Embassy of Japan in Bangladesh and Immigration Services Agency guidance, confirmed 26 Sep 2026
Confirmed policy detail
Work-hour compliance just got stricter — plan around it, not just the 28-hour cap.
Since April 2026, Japanese language schools have been required to review each student's work situation roughly every three months — checking employer, duties and hours worked, and reporting suspected violations to immigration. This doesn't change the 28-hour weekly cap itself, but it means the informal "nobody really checks" assumption some students carry in no longer holds. Keep your own record of hours and employers from week one. Confirmed via immigration-compliance guidance current as of September 2026.

Scholarships worth actually applying for.
Japan's flagship government scholarship is fully open to Bangladeshi applicants, through two separate application routes.

MEXT Scholarship — Embassy Recommendation
Government of Japan · Full tuition waiver, ¥117k/mo (undergrad) or ¥143k/mo (research student), round-trip airfare
Apply via Embassy of Japan in Dhaka · deadline typically May–June
MEXT Scholarship — University Recommendation
Individual partner universities · Same benefits, applied for directly once you have an offer
Apply directly through your chosen university
JASSO Scholarship for Privately Financed Students
Japan Student Services Organization · ~¥48,000/month for up to 1 year
Nominated by your university — not a direct application, and not guaranteed
