01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "the Irish are friendly and welcoming" — the specific, slightly awkward details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"If someone says 'we should hang out sometime,' when do we?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Possibly never — Irish small talk is warm but often non-committal. "We should grab a coffee" is frequently just friendliness, not a real invitation. If someone actually names a day and time, that's the real signal.
"Is the weather actually as unpredictable as people joke?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Genuinely, yes — four seasons in one afternoon is a real thing, not an exaggeration. A waterproof jacket you actually wear beats an umbrella most days; Atlantic wind makes umbrellas short-lived.
"Do I need to tip everywhere, like in the US?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No — tipping isn't automatic. 10% at a sit-down restaurant if you were happy with the service is generous and appreciated; nothing's expected at a pub bar or café counter.
"How urgent is it to register with a doctor (GP)?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Sort it in week one — unlike some countries, Ireland's public healthcare isn't automatically free for non-EU students. Your mandatory private insurance is what actually covers you, so confirm what it covers and where before you need it, not after.
"Is the accommodation situation really as tight as people say?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Genuinely, yes, especially in Dublin — university accommodation is limited and private rentals move fast. Start searching the moment you have an offer, not after your visa is approved.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
Ireland's student immigration permission, in plain language.
Not the full legal text — the numbers that actually decide whether you qualify.
WHAT YOU'LL NEED TO SHOW
€10,000
≈ ৳14.1L · Funds you or your sponsor can access, shown as bank statements
Of that, at least €6,000 must already be paid to your university
New since 30 June 2025 — your acceptance letter must explicitly confirm this payment before a visa can be granted. The filled squares are what must be pre-paid; the outlined squares just need to be shown as available.
Visa (long-stay 'D') fee: €60 single-entry, separate from a further €300 registration fee paid after arrival to receive your actual residence permission (Stamp 2).
Private medical insurance is mandatory, covering at least €25,000 for in-hospital treatment — Ireland's public healthcare isn't automatically free for non-EU students.
Post-study work: the Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G) grants 12 months for Bachelor's graduates, and 12 months renewable once — up to 24 months total — for Master's and PhD graduates.
Checked against Irish Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) guidance, confirmed 2 Sep 2026
Confirmed policy change
You now have to pay before you're granted the visa to pay for.
Since 30 June 2025, every student visa applicant must show at least €6,000 already paid toward tuition — or the full fee, if it's below €6,000 — confirmed explicitly on the university's acceptance letter. This flips the usual order: in most other countries a visa comes before a large payment. Budget for this transfer well before you apply, not after.
TWO MORE THINGS WORTH KNOWING
Work rights: 20 hours/week during term, rising to 40 hours/week during 15 June–15 September and 15 December–15 January.
From 2026, bringing a spouse or dependent children is generally restricted to postgraduate students at Master's level or above.
