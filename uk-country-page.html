01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "the UK is diverse and welcoming" — the specific, slightly awkward details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"Late means 5 minutes early, right?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Lectures start on the dot. Arriving even a few minutes late to a seminar draws looks — "on time" here often means five minutes early.
"Can I just nip in front of a short queue?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Don't. The queue is sacred — cutting one, even by accident, is the fastest way to get a very pointed silent stare.
"Why does everyone talk about the rain?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Weather is the default icebreaker. Complaining about it together is basically a bonding ritual — join in, don't just nod along.
"Do I need to tip everywhere?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No. Tipping isn't automatic — 10–12.5% at a sit-down restaurant is generous; nothing's expected at a café or pub bar.
"When do I sort out a doctor?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Week one, not someday. Registering with a GP early means the NHS is free at the point of use once you're in the system.
"Is Freshers' Week actually worth it?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes — student societies are how most people actually make friends here. Go to more events than feels comfortable in week one.

Visa application fee: £558 (from 8 April 2026), separate from tuition, living costs and the health surcharge.
Post-study work: the Graduate Route currently grants 2 years for Bachelor's/Master's graduates, 3 years for PhD — see the policy note.
Checked against UK Home Office guidance, confirmed 13 Aug 2026
Confirmed policy change
The Graduate Route is shortening — apply before it does.
Graduating and applying for the Graduate Route on or before 31 December 2026 still gets you the full 2-year post-study work window. From 1 January 2027, new applicants get 18 months instead. If your course finishes in 2026, there's no reason to delay applying once results are confirmed — this is a dated, confirmed change, not a proposal.
