01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "Australia is laid-back and friendly" — the specific, slightly awkward details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"Wait, will I have Christmas in summer?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes — the seasons here run exactly opposite to Dhaka's. December is peak summer, July is the coldest month. Your first "winter" in Australia will feel a lot like your first "summer" felt at home, just relabeled.
"Is the wildlife actually a daily concern?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Not really, in the cities. Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane are as unremarkable wildlife-wise as any big city — the spider and snake stories are mostly a rural and outback thing, not a walking-to-lectures thing.
"How seriously do they take sun protection?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Genuinely seriously — Australia has one of the highest UV indexes and skin cancer rates in the world. "Slip, slop, slap" (shirt, sunscreen, hat) is a real, constantly-repeated public health message, not a joke.
"Is it really as laid-back as people say?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Socially, mostly yes — first names for lecturers and casual dress are the norm. Academically, no — assignment deadlines and referencing rules are enforced strictly, and "she'll be right" doesn't apply to late submissions.
"Is a car actually necessary here?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Depends entirely on the city. Sydney and Melbourne have genuinely good public transport for campus life; Perth, Brisbane and especially Gold Coast are far more spread out and car-dependent day to day.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The Student visa (Subclass 500), in plain language.
Not the full legal text — the numbers that actually decide whether you qualify, and a Bangladesh-specific change worth knowing before you apply.
WHAT YOU'LL NEED TO SHOW
$29,710
≈ ৳26L · 12 months' living costs — separate from tuition and travel, reviewed and raised annually
HOW FUNDING PROOF ACTUALLY WORKS
No fixed holding period, but funds must be genuine and traceable — bank statements, a loan letter, or scholarship evidence, all cross-checked against your actual financial history, not just a snapshot balance.
Visa application fee: $2,500 (from 1 July 2026, up from $2,000) — non-refundable even if the application is refused.
Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is mandatory for your full visa length: roughly $500–700/year for a single applicant, arranged before the visa is granted.
Post-study work: the Temporary Graduate visa (485) lets eligible graduates work in Australia — length depends on your qualification. Applying costs a separate $5,750, tripled in the last year alone.
Checked against Department of Home Affairs guidance, confirmed 2 Sep 2026
Confirmed policy change — Bangladesh-specific
Bangladesh moved to the highest-scrutiny visa tier, without warning.
On 8 January 2026, in an unusual out-of-cycle decision, Australia's Department of Home Affairs reclassified Bangladesh — along with India, Nepal, and Bhutan — from Evidence Level 2 to Evidence Level 3, the strictest integrity tier in the student visa system. This came just months after Bangladesh had been granted Level 1, the lowest-risk category, in September 2025 — a genuine reversal, not a gradual shift. In practice, this means more documentation (bank statements covering several months, verified source-of-funds evidence, academic transcripts checked directly against issuing institutions), more manual review, and processing times that can run to 8 weeks instead of the usual 3. It doesn't mean a genuine application is more likely to be refused — it means it takes longer and needs to be more thoroughly documented from the start. Apply earlier than you think you need to.
