01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "China is ancient and modern at once" — the specific, practical details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"Do I need a VPN before I even land?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes. Google, Gmail, WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram and YouTube are all blocked. Set up a VPN before you arrive — buying one after landing is harder than it sounds.
"Can I just pay with my foreign card everywhere?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Barely. China runs on WeChat Pay and Alipay, not cash or cards — link a foreign card to one of these apps as soon as you land, or even a metro ticket becomes a struggle.
"Will everyone speak English?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Rarely, outside university campuses and the biggest cities. Basic Mandarin — or a translation app with offline mode — makes daily life dramatically easier.
"Do I need to tip anywhere?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No. Tipping isn't part of the culture at all — it can come across as awkward or confusing to restaurant staff rather than generous.
"Do I need to register with the police when I arrive?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes — anyone staying somewhere that isn't a hotel must register at the local Public Security Bureau within 24 hours. Most universities handle this for on-campus dorms; off-campus housing means doing it yourself.
"Is 'saving face' really such a big deal?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Genuinely, yes. Public criticism or direct confrontation — even meant helpfully — can embarrass someone in front of others. Raising a problem privately, one-on-one, almost always lands better.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The X visa, in plain language.
Not the full legal text — the numbers and deadlines that actually decide whether you qualify.
YOUR VISA TYPE, DECODED
Residence permit required
Apply within 30 days of arrival at your local Public Security Bureau — it's tied to your programme length and renews with your enrolment, not before.
REGISTER WITHIN 30 DAYS OF ARRIVAL
Miss this window and you risk fines or an invalid stay — the Public Security Bureau near your campus handles it, not the embassy.
Visa fee: typically USD 25–90, depending on nationality and reciprocity, plus a residence permit fee of roughly RMB 400–800 once you're registered in China.
Work rights while studying: up to 8 hours a week (40/month) during term, 16 hours a week (80/month) during breaks — only after your first year enrolled, and off-campus work must be registered with the local Exit-Entry bureau within 10 days.
Post-study work: no general graduate visa — most cities require a job offer and 2 years' prior work experience for a standard Z visa. Shanghai is the exception — see the policy note.
Checked against Shanghai Municipal Government and embassy guidance, confirmed 23 Sep 2026
Confirmed policy detail
Shanghai's Category C permit is the most realistic post-study bridge — if you qualify.
Most Chinese cities still require two years of prior overseas work experience before granting a standard Z work visa. Shanghai's Category C permit removes that requirement for Master's graduates of any Chinese university, and for Bachelor's graduates of Shanghai institutions in designated zones — apply within two years of graduating, at or above the local minimum wage. It's Shanghai-specific, not a national rule, so where you study — or plan to work — still matters. Confirmed by Shanghai Municipal Government guidance, updated 30 April 2026.
