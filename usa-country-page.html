01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
Not "the US is diverse and dynamic" — the specific, slightly awkward details that actually catch first-year students off guard.
"Do I need to tip everywhere?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Yes, and more than most countries — 18–20% at a sit-down restaurant is standard, not generous. Tipping less than that reads as an actual insult, not just thrift.
"Is health insurance really mandatory?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Nearly every university requires proof of coverage to enroll at all — there's no NHS equivalent. A lapse in coverage can turn one ER visit into a bill running into the thousands.
"Can I call my professor by their first name?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Often, yes — plenty of professors invite it outright, especially in grad school. It's not disrespect here, it's the norm at many schools. Check the syllabus on day one to be sure which kind you've got.
"Does 'How are you?' need a real answer?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
No — it's usually just hello. "Good, you?" is the expected reply, not an invitation to actually explain your week. Save the real answer for someone who asks twice.
"Why does everyone talk about credit scores?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Arriving with zero US credit history genuinely affects apartment leases, phone plans, even some part-time jobs. A secured student credit card in month one, paid off in full each month, solves most of this by year two.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The F-1 visa, in plain language.
Not the full legal text — the numbers that actually decide whether you qualify, and one very recent change worth knowing before you apply.
ILLUSTRATIVE FIRST-YEAR COST YOUR I-20 MIGHT SHOW
$44,000
≈ ৳53.9L · the exact figure is whatever your I-20 states, not a government-set amount
HOW FUNDING PROOF ACTUALLY WORKS
There's no fixed holding period like some other countries require. Your school sets the required amount on your I-20, and you show it via a bank statement or a notarized affidavit of support — most schools want it dated within the last 6 months.
SEVIS I-901 fee: $350, paid once your school issues your I-20, before you can pay the visa fee.
Visa application (DS-160/MRV) fee: $185 — separate payment, separate system. $535 total in government fees before tuition.
Post-study work: standard OPT gives 12 months; a STEM-designated degree adds a 24-month extension — up to 36 months total.
Checked against DHS and Study in the States guidance, confirmed 2 Sep 2026
Confirmed policy change — takes effect in days, not months
"Duration of Status" ends September 15, 2026.
For more than three decades, F-1 students could stay in the US for as long as they maintained valid status, with no fixed end date. That changes on September 15, 2026: DHS now admits F-1 students for a fixed period tied to their I-20 program length, capped at 4 years, plus a 30-day arrival window and 30-day departure window. Needing more time — a longer program, a program change, or starting OPT after travel abroad — now means filing a formal Extension of Stay with USCIS, a step that didn't functionally exist before. Students already in the US on the old open-ended status get a transition allowance if they file for OPT or STEM OPT by 18 March 2027. This is a genuinely new layer of paperwork to plan around, not a minor technicality — and it's still subject to Congressional review, so it's worth checking DHS's own page for the latest status before you rely on any of this.
