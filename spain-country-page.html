01 / THE WORLD YOU'RE STEPPING INTO
Small things nobody puts in a brochure.
The practical details that tend to catch first-year students off guard, not a general portrait of the country.
"When do people actually eat?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Later than you expect. Lunch (la comida) is the main meal, usually from about 14:00, and many cafés serve a fixed-price menú del día on weekdays. Dinner is often after 21:00, and outside tourist areas many kitchens do not open for it much earlier.
"How do people greet each other?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Friends and classmates commonly greet with a kiss on each cheek (dos besos), between women and between a man and a woman; men often shake hands or hug. If you are unsure, a handshake at the first meeting is fine, and you will see quickly what your group does.
"What are the padrón and the TIE?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
The padrón (empadronamiento) is your address registration at the town hall, and many offices ask for a certificate of it. The TIE is the foreigner identity card issued to students staying longer than six months; you apply for it within one month of arriving, so book the appointment early.
"Is halal food easy to find?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
In Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and other big cities, yes: there are halal butchers, grocers and restaurants, mostly in neighbourhoods with large Muslim communities. Pork is central to the cuisine, though, and ham or lard turns up in croquetas, bocadillos and stews, so ask about ingredients. Smaller cities have less choice.
"Can I get by with English only?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
For many English-taught master's programmes, classes are fine. Daily life is another matter: landlords, banks, offices and most friendships run in Spanish, and in Catalonia and Valencia you will also hear Catalan or Valencian. Most bachelor's degrees are taught in Spanish, so check each programme's teaching language.
"What happens in August?"
Tap to reveal
Actually
Things slow down. Many small shops and restaurants close for a few weeks, offices and university administrations work reduced hours, and Madrid regularly passes 33°C. Do your paperwork before July, and do not plan on getting anything signed in August.

06 / THE RULES OF THE ROUTE
The Spanish student visa, in plain language.
For stays over 90 days you apply for a national student visa at the Spanish embassy in Dhaka. These are the numbers and deadlines that decide whether you qualify.
MONTHLY FUNDS YOU HAVE TO SHOW, BY HOUSEHOLD
€600 / month
AFTER GRADUATION: 24 MONTHS TO LOOK FOR WORK, NOT EXTENDABLE
Each square is one month of the post-study job-search permit. It lets you stay and look for work or set up a business, but not to work until you switch to a work authorisation. The permit needs a completed course at degree level or above, and you apply in the 60 days before your study permit expires.
Where to apply: in person at the Embassy of Spain in Dhaka, by appointment. BLS Dhaka handles only short-stay study visas of up to 90 days, so it is not the route for a degree.
Visa fee: the standard national-visa fee is €80 (about ৳11,000), but Bangladesh has a different reciprocity rate, so confirm the exact amount with the embassy. The €90 shown on BLS's page is the short-stay fee, not this one.
Funds to show: 100% of the monthly IPREM, which is €600 in 2026 (about ৳82,900), plus 75% for a first family member and 50% for each additional one. The amount can be lower if you have prepaid the whole stay's accommodation. You also need health insurance from a Spanish-authorised insurer covering all the public system's risks.
Documents for stays over 180 days include a police certificate covering the last 5 years and a medical certificate, legalised or apostilled and translated into Spanish where the embassy asks. Your passport needs 2 blank pages and must be under 10 years old.
Decision time: the legal period is 1 month from the day after you submit, and it can be extended. Collect the visa within 2 months of being notified. It covers a 90-day entry period, and you apply for the TIE within 1 month of arriving.
Working while you study: up to 30 hours a week, as long as it does not clash with classes and, in general, within the same autonomous community. For higher-education students the authorisation is automatic (rules in force since 20 May 2025).
Checked against Spanish government and BLS Dhaka published pages, 11 Oct 2026
After graduation · 24 months
The 24-month post-study permit is for searching, not working.
After completing studies in Spain at degree level or above (EQF level 6 or higher), you can apply for a residence authorisation to look for work or start a business. It lasts 24 months and cannot be extended. You apply in the 60 days before your study permit expires, and show funds equal to 100% of IPREM (50% if accommodation is prepaid). The Ministry's information sheet says you are not authorised to work while you hold it; once you find a job, you have to switch to a work authorisation. Another route is a highly qualified worker permit, reported at a salary of over €43,000 a year (secondary source). Rules like these get updated, so read the current Ministry text before you decide.
